# SemaFrame launch, hero, and RealityOps videos

This folder contains the reproducible launch-video source. The screenshots and interaction frames are captured from a real local Agent handshake and real Workspace MCP transactions; the edit only adds pacing, framing, explanatory typography, and an original generated score.

The RealityOps film adds a second, end-to-end story: an Agent builds a clearly labelled synthetic brownfield pump-room baseline, rejects an unsafe retrofit placement, corrects it, publishes a reusable multipart model, connects telemetry and 2D controls to the 3D state, preserves the revisioned result, reopens it, and exports standard artifacts. The baseline is procedural because no field capture was supplied; the film never presents it as a real scan.

The RealityOps V2 proof cut repackages that technical evidence for a first-time viewer. In its opening seconds it establishes the ordinary-language problem—a factory needs a backup pump—and defines SemaFrame as an AI-readable, AI-operable 2D+3D workspace. Twelve silent-first beats then show the request, editable multipart construction, spatial reading, atomic collision rejection, safe correction, deterministic telemetry and 2D-to-3D control, Undo/Redo/Save/Reopen, and scoped OpenUSD/STEP export. The 36-second landscape and 32-second portrait editions are authored separately in Simplified Chinese and English, with matching accessibility captions. They make no claim of a field scan, live plant telemetry, continuous engineering simulation, or autonomous certified design.

The miniature-city emergency-corridor hero is the general-audience proof of SemaFrame's executable-space model. An explicitly authorized Agent reads an exact dispatch snapshot and the live SemaFrame Spatial Graph without changing Workspace revision. The host defines the required emergency effects and candidate safe regions; a real isolated Codex CLI run identifies the blockers, assigns vehicles to bays, and authors exact endpoint coordinates through a strict JSON schema. SemaFrame then rejects unsafe proposals and preflights every accepted endpoint at that exact revision before compiling the editable button routes. One real pointer click executes the validated response atomically, Undo/Redo restores it, and the real Save/Open path preserves the complete route definitions. The city and feed are deterministic synthetic inputs, not a surveyed municipality or a network refresh.

The V3 proof cut tells the same verified story without requiring sound. Ten explicit visual beats establish the crisis, goal, spatial read, rejected collision, five safe placements, single human confirmation, city response, successful arrival, editability, and SemaFrame identity. Its generated nonverbal score is supplemental: the machine-readable visual contract sets `silentFirst` to `true` and `audioRequiredForComprehension` to `false`. Landscape and portrait are separately composed rather than mechanically cropped.

The V4 product-comprehension cut keeps that verified capture but answers the missing first-time-viewer question in the opening five seconds: **what is SemaFrame, and what is the AI actually doing?** Twelve visual beats now separate product definition, the human request, dispatch-data reading, object/rule/action understanding, collision rejection, safe planning, approval, atomic execution, arrival, and persistent editability. Scene-native semantic callouts point to the real captured ambulance, protected tree collider, switchable signal, 11 committed targets, and revision history. The final distinction is explicit: this is not a pre-rendered animation; the Agent is modifying a saved, human-editable 2D+3D workspace. The English landscape and portrait editions redraw the two baked-in viewport labels—the dispatch chart and emergency-route button—in the capture's own coordinate space, so the evidence remains aligned through every crop. Sound remains optional.

`demo:capture` starts isolated local Vite, Gateway, Chrome, and MCP processes. It uses a deterministic inline snapshot rather than depending on an external feed. Connection and transaction capabilities are not written to video assets; the displayed connection URL is replaced with a non-authorizing placeholder before capture.

`demo:capture:realityops` uses the same browser-authoritative path and records actual UI transitions, collision evidence, model publication, data/action wiring, save/reopen, and USDA/STEP export. It writes only compact non-secret evidence to `video/public/realityops/evidence.json`; local rendered binaries remain under ignored `artifacts/`.

`demo:capture:living-room` records the general-audience scene through the real Agent gateway: an editable multipart sofa is rejected atomically at a protected doorway, moved to a validated position, and followed by a real viewport button that routes one press to five 3D state changes. Its non-secret acceptance evidence is written to `video/public/living-room/evidence.json`.

`demo:capture:emergency-city` is an independent browser-authoritative pipeline. It requests `effect:data_read` explicitly, treats snapshot and component strings as untrusted data, and gives an isolated, ephemeral, read-only Codex CLI planner the authoritative snapshot plus spatial graph. The planner receives visible candidate bays and the host's required effects, but no vehicle-to-bay assignment or exact endpoint coordinates; there is no automatic fixture or hardcoded fallback. The host requires mission completeness, runs `query_spatial_placement` for every model endpoint at the plan's source revision, and only then compiles the routes. Planner context, schema, prompt, raw JSONL trace, plan, truth-window events, host-validation receipt, and run identity are SHA-256 linked under `video/public/emergency-city/planner/`. Remotion uses all 1,110 resulting Workspace images one-to-one at 30 fps; it does not recreate the city or vehicle motion. `demo:verify:emergency-city` rechecks those hashes, the real click/Undo/Redo/Save/Open receipts, exact frame contract, generated score, 37-second H.264/BT.709/YUV420p delivery, loudness, poster, and final spatial/physics status.

The live capture defaults to the explicit `gpt-5.5` model ID, which is supported by the pinned local Codex CLI used for this release. Override it without changing source by setting `SEMAFRAME_DEMO_PLANNER_MODEL`; planner failure never selects the deterministic test fixture implicitly. `demo:test:emergency-city-planner` uses fixtures and a fake Codex executable to verify the fail-closed integration contract without making a model call. `demo:release:emergency-city` runs the complete live planning, capture, render, poster, and verification pipeline.

V3 reuses that browser-authoritative capture and evidence chain; it does not recreate the city in Remotion. `video/emergency-city-v3.visual-contract.json` defines both timelines, safe areas, exact text bounds, and every required comprehension beat. `demo:render:emergency-city:v3` generates the optional score, renders both aspect ratios, and normalizes both deliverables to H.264, BT.709 limited-range YUV420p, 30 fps, AAC, fast-start, and the target loudness. `demo:verify:emergency-city:v3` then checks the live-model/no-fallback evidence, revision isolation, five accepted preflights, 11-action single-click commit, zero final collisions, Undo/Redo/Save/Open survival, source-frame contract, both media files and posters, safe visual layers, and black/frozen-frame limits. `demo:release:emergency-city:v3` runs planner and verifier tests before a fresh live capture, both renders, both posters, and final verification.

V4 is a new edit over the same browser-authoritative evidence, not a replacement capture. `video/emergency-city-v4.visual-contract.json` freezes both aspect-ratio timelines, the five-second product-comprehension deadline, visible copy, semantic overlay bounds, source-frame mappings, transcript timing, claim boundaries, and composition-source hashes. `demo:verify:emergency-city:v4` revalidates the complete evidence chain plus the two encoded masters, posters, safe areas, source evolution, optional-score delivery, and silent-first contract. The supplemental labels describe verified endpoint preflight and final state; they do not claim continuous swept-path simulation or a live municipal feed.

RealityOps V2 likewise edits the browser-authoritative RealityOps capture rather than simulating the product in Remotion. `video/realityops-v2.visual-contract.json` freezes the dual-aspect timelines, comprehension deadline, visible copy, source mappings and hashes, safe areas, captions, and claim boundaries. `demo:verify:realityops:v2` checks all 340 capture frames, atomic revision rejection, corrected zero-conflict state, bounded physics receipt, editable source/instance identity, the real one-press panel-to-beacon event, Undo/Redo/Save/Open persistence, scoped USDA/STEP contents, both encoded masters, both posters, audio delivery, and black/frozen-frame limits. The all-in-one release command starts with a fresh browser capture so a stale edit cannot masquerade as current product evidence.

`video/english-demo-gallery.visual-contract.json` binds the five English compositions, posters, captions, component sources, supporting contracts, output names, and delivery properties into one release surface. `demo:test:english-gallery` and the verifier's static mode catch registration, source-hash, timeline, language, README-link, and truth-boundary drift before rendering. `demo:render:readme-posters` deterministically derives the three tracked 1280×720 JPEG gallery covers from the full-resolution Remotion posters. The full `demo:verify:english-gallery` gate then probes all five MP4s and posters plus the three README covers for exact dimensions and timing, H.264/AAC/BT.709 delivery, fast-start, freshness, and black/frozen failures. `scripts/render-english-demo-qa-stills.mjs` renders eight representative frames from every composition plus contact sheets for direct visual review.

The demonstration is intentionally bounded. Its miniature city and dispatch feed are deterministic synthetic inputs, not a surveyed digital twin, live municipal traffic, a swept-path traffic model, or an emergency-system certification. It proves the product workflow and endpoint collision-preflight contract on editable scene data; it does not claim real-world route safety without field geometry, domain rules, and independent validation.

The process cut uses the staged immersive frame sequences from that capture directly. It opens in the workspace, keeps explanatory UI to one short bilingual line, shows collision rejection and correction only when those states actually exist, and limits save/reopen/export UI evidence to brief hard cuts.

The scores in `video/public/audio/` are generated locally by the matching scripts, are project-owned, and are intentionally ignored by Git.
